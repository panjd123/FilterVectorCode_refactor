# Independent review of ML-UNG

Reviewed artifact: `/tmp/mlung-cold-c0-20261006/paper.pdf`, 23 pages. Review date: 2026-10-06. Page references below use the PDF's printed page numbers. I read the main manuscript and Appendices A–E. I did not inspect project files, source code, previous reviews, or author notes, and did not execute experiments. This is a scientific assessment of the supplied manuscript, not verification of the implementation.

**Recommendation: Reject. Confidence: 4/5 (high).** The central design is understandable and potentially useful, but the submitted evidence does not yet establish a sufficiently general or competitive advance. In addition, the specified overlay seeding can lose access to eligible residual groups independently of finite search budget. Addressing these concerns requires additional algorithmic analysis and experiments, not just rewriting. This recommendation is independent of submission-format issues.

The following are diagnostic scores, not an official PVLDB rubric. Higher is better.

| Dimension | Score | Rationale |
|---|---:|---|
| Novelty/significance | 3/5 | A plausible new combination of entry contracts and certified block graphs addresses an important problem, but its advantage over strong existing alternatives and its generality are unestablished. |

| Empirical support | 2/5 | Useful internal factorials and honest negative results, but only one dataset tests the main proposed combination; no external competitive baselines, confounded workload construction, and no graph-quality comparison for construction speedups. |

**Contribution type and understanding of the paper.** I assess this as a regular research paper with a systems/algorithm-design contribution. It proposes a new index/search organization and evaluates a prototype, rather than principally surveying existing methods or introducing a benchmark. PVLDB's systems-paper description permits novelty in an effective combination of existing techniques; the paper need not invent each primitive. Conversely, the three short structural propositions do not make this primarily a foundations paper with experiments serving only as illustrations.

The first proposed mechanism replaces UNG's minimal observed-superset entry contract and containment-cover topology with a terminal-prefix forest and its covering frontier. The measured provider obtains eligible groups through label bitmaps and removes groups having an eligible terminal ancestor. The second mechanism independently partitions trie regions into blocks, using containment of the query in a block's root prefix to authorize graph navigation across its direct-member exact groups. Search maintains physical-level tags and uses only the matching adjacency. GPU construction and the size/degree-based DRH configuration heuristic support this design.

The main empirical claims are narrow enough to identify: on the Amazon workloads, the matched Trie base is 2.77–18.43 times faster than the optimized LNG base at the three lowest batch-mean selectivities, but loses at other workloads; adding overlays changes which organization wins. The full factorial demonstrates substantial interactions among entry provider, base topology, and overlay topology. DRH transfer is uneven, and construction time falls for some GPU-assisted builder profiles.

**What works well.**

- The joint entry/connectivity perspective is a useful contribution. Sections 2–3 and Figure 2 explain why substituting prefix edges requires a different entry frontier. Proposition 1 states a label-plane result and explicitly avoids claiming that it guarantees approximate vector recall.
- Independent block partitions, direct membership, authorization, and ownership are specified clearly in Sections 4–5. Distinguishing an emission threshold from a maximum block size, and independent levels from nested partitions, prevents several easy misunderstandings.
- Proposition 2's eligibility induction is convincing under its stated invariants. A common-prefix certificate is simple, but it provides a clean reason to remove per-neighbor predicate checks inside authorized regions. I found no counterexample to this safety proposition.
- The evaluation does not hide regressions. It reports no-crossing cases, distinguishes a workload-wise oracle from a deployable policy, exposes DRH's VariousImg failure, and separates instrumented stage times from primary batch throughput. Tables 10–14 and the appendix curves provide useful evidence beyond selected winners.
- The paper correctly separates structural reload checks from query quality and recognizes that the GPU builders are different complete profiles. That candor helps assessment, even though the missing quality evidence remains consequential.

**Material concerns.**

**1. Retagging can eliminate the only route to eligible residual groups, even without a bounded-search approximation.** Sections 4.2 and 5.1–5.2, pp. 4–5; Algorithm 4; Proposition 3, p. 6.

The paper says residual groups remain represented by the base, but representation in storage does not establish reachability in the executed search. An LNG minimal entry can cover supersets outside its prefix subtree through base-level LNG edges. Retagging that entry to its authorized overlay removes those base expansions, and the level-local rule supplies no way back.

Consider the following instance under label order `a < b`. There are 11 vectors with label set `{b}` and 10 with `{a,b}`. Use one ungated overlay with threshold `T=10`, an LNG base, and query `Q={b}`. Algorithm 2 emits a block at prefix `{b}` because its mass is 11. The `{a,b}` branch has mass 10, so it emits no block; these 10 vectors are residual base members. The sole LNG-minimal group entry is `{b}`. Section 5.1 retags its seed to level 1, and the only block seed also belongs to the `{b}` block. Therefore every initialized state is at level 1. That overlay has no block containing `{a,b}`, and level-local expansion can never use the base edge `{b} -> {a,b}`. The 10 residual vectors cannot be reached.

This persists with an unlimited queue, fully connected local graphs, and a correctly materialized base transition. Choose the 10 residual vectors as the true nearest neighbors: Recall@10 is then zero despite all returned/scored vectors being eligible. Thus the example does **not** refute Proposition 2, and it does not refute the prefix-base Proposition 1. It shows a distinct structural defect in the advertised LNG-base overlay configurations, which are extensively evaluated and underpin the held-out policy study.

A repair could retain appropriate base states, seed uncovered residual frontiers, or establish another coverage invariant. I do not prescribe a particular repair, because its cost changes the main tradeoff. At minimum, the paper must characterize this failure explicitly and distinguish it from capacity eviction or approximate local connectivity. The repeated no-crossing behavior of LNG-base overlays in Tables 10–13 makes that distinction empirically important. New coverage analysis and search-quality experiments are required.

**2. The evaluation establishes comparisons within one implementation family, not competitive significance.** Sections 7.4 and 8.1–8.2, pp. 8–10; related work, pp. 10–11; Tables 4–5 and 10–11.

All reported query competitors are the authors' LNG/Trie/overlay variants. Plain is an optimized provider on a common refactored execution path, not an independently validated reproduction of UNG's original query performance. There is no measured ACORN, Curator, Filtered-DiskANN where semantically applicable, filter-first exact search, or suitable unfiltered ANN control for empty predicates. The related work explains that several of these systems address exactly the relevant low-selectivity/composite-predicate regime, but does not experimentally position ML-UNG against them.

This omission is especially material because Plain falls to 0.79 QPS on the 99% workload, which contains 697 empty predicates out of 1,000 (p. 8). Table 9 requires a Plain queue capacity of 400,000 there. The default queue has linear-in-capacity work even for some rejected insertions (Section 5.3). Large gains against this baseline may reflect a very expensive execution path, not a competitive filtered-ANN frontier. I am not asserting that the numbers are incorrect; the manuscript lacks the controls needed to interpret them.

The needed evidence includes at least strong applicable external baselines and exact scanning, compatible filtering semantics, documented tuning/resource budgets, and verification that Plain is a credible UNG control. A standard efficient queue or an appropriate queue sensitivity study would help establish whether the proposed structural gains survive this implementation choice. These are missing experiments, not a request to phrase the existing speedups more cautiously.

**3. The core contribution is evaluated only on Amazon, and the selectivity sweep is heavily coupled to predicate composition and label order.** Sections 7.3 and 8.1, pp. 7–9; Tables 3–6.

Genome, Reviews, and VariousImg are held out only for DRH with the LNG base. They do not test the main Trie-base frontier plus overlay combination. Amazon has 482,387 exact groups for 602,453 vectors, an unusually fragmented regime that directly motivates this design. There is no evidence establishing how the proposed combination behaves when exact groups are larger, labels are less fragmented, or vector/label correlation differs.

The Amazon sweep also does not hold predicate structure fixed while changing selectivity. Several batches replace predicates with the same `{1}` predicate; label 1 covers 96.702% of the database, and the 99% batch combines this predicate with empty filters. Individual selectivities in the nominal 5% batch span 0.0033% to 96.702%. The manuscript discloses this and calls the measurements workload families, which is appropriate. Nevertheless, an aggregate batch Recall@10 of 0.90 can conceal very different recall on the constituent predicate classes, and a batch-mean selectivity is insufficient to explain the observed winners.

There is also a mechanism-specific concern: sorting integer labels ascending places this overwhelmingly common label first. Prefix certificates and block membership depend on that order, although the logical query semantics do not. Renaming labels could change the index considerably. Please measure representative label orders/permutations, report frontier and block-authorization distributions, and separate `{1}`, empty, and remaining predicates in both recall and cost. Test the central method on multiple datasets and on workloads whose per-query selectivity/cardinality structure is controlled or realistically characterized. These additions are needed for generality, not merely a better figure caption.

**4. The experiments do not separate cheaper entry data structures from the changed entry/topology contract.** Section 3.2, pp. 2–3; Section 7.4, p. 8; Figure 1 and Section 8.2.

The new provider uses compressed postings plus ancestor checks; the LNG providers use a trie walk plus pairwise containment reduction. Thus the large discovery reductions combine a different candidate-access method with removing global minimality checks. Calling the 0L experiment a matched-provider system comparison is accurate, but it leaves the central causal explanation unresolved.

Useful compatible controls exist. The prefix frontier can seed an LNG graph: it contains all LNG-minimal entries and every seed is eligible. Compare that with both the standard LNG provider on LNG and the prefix provider on Trie. Also compare trie-walk prefix candidate discovery without global reduction against the bitmap implementation, or another appropriate shared candidate-access control. Report eligible-group count, actual frontier count, minimal-entry count, ancestor probes, and seed distances. The existing profile reports downstream distances but not enough of these variables to determine which proposed mechanism earns the gain. A full arbitrary cross-product is unnecessary; targeted valid controls would answer the question.

**5. Construction speedups lack the output-quality and resource comparison needed for a usable-index claim.** Sections 6.1–6.2 and 7.5, pp. 6–8; Section 8.4, p. 9; Tables 7 and 19–21, pp. 11, 18–19.

CPU Vamana, sampled candidates, GPU NN-Descent-style refinement, different pruning/completion paths, and TF32 selection can produce different graphs. Structural membership/ownership validation is insufficient to establish comparable query quality. The manuscript explicitly says query-quality equivalence has not been measured. Consequently the 11.06-times hierarchy construction ratio and the 1.35-times composed ratio compare output-producing procedures without demonstrating that their outputs provide comparable recall/throughput. The composed total is also a sum of separate stage medians, not a measured integrated build; the paper labels that correctly.

Measure the resulting indexes' recall–throughput curves and sizes under the same search contract, and compare construction cost at a defensible quality target. If GPU acceleration is intended only as an implementation observation, retain that limited scope and avoid treating these ratios as evidence of an equivalent faster index builder. Separately, query-time index memory, bitmap/ancestor metadata, adjacency sizes, and per-worker workspace are missing across the main configurations. Peak builder RSS is not a substitute, especially with 100 workers and very large candidate queues.

**6. The policy study is an honest negative result, but does not validate an effective configuration method.** Section 6.3, p. 7; Section 7.3, p. 8; Section 8.3 and Tables 6, 15–17, pp. 9–10, 17.

The `T + N/T` objective is a structural proxy, not a model derived from the actual graph-search costs; `N/T` is not even the emitted block count, as the manuscript explains. The degree-ratio growth and topology threshold are plausible heuristics, but their connection to navigation performance is weak. All datasets produce two overlays, so the experiment does not test the claimed depth-selection behavior across different resulting depths.

Moreover, the five manual alternatives include a one-overlay plan under a gate requiring an authorized level of at least 2. This candidate always executes the base, so it is a fallback control rather than an evaluated one-overlay design. The paper discloses this, but the comparison still explores a restricted neighborhood of the proposed rule. DRH loses nearly fivefold on VariousImg; the revised gate remains about threefold below its same-cohort Plain result. These are valuable observations about failure, not evidence for a generally effective automatic plan. A deployable selector is not mandatory for an oracle design-space study, but the paper should keep that study distinct from demonstrating a usable automatic policy. The strongest fixed Trie-base configuration has not been evaluated on the held-out datasets at all.

**7. Reproducibility and uncertainty are insufficient for several fine-grained conclusions.** Sections 7.2–7.5, pp. 7–8; Appendix A, p. 13; Tables 4 and 10–13.

The hardware, repeat structure, recall crossing rule, shared caps, and counter definitions are helpful. However, the supplied manuscript refers to “the repository,” artifact manifests, workload directories, and a partial aggregate digest without providing a retrievable artifact location. I cannot determine whether a separate submission portal contains the material; I do not infer that it does not exist. From the supplied PDF alone, I cannot reconstruct the vector/label preprocessing, predicate replacement masks and seeds, complete graph-building parameters, stored entry-point selection, or exact search-budget grid and selected budget/actual recall for each configuration. Table 9 gives Plain's crossing and caps, not these complete operating points.

Two warm repeats of the same frozen query order support descriptive timing observations but do not establish uncertainty across workloads, build randomness, or process conditions. Several reported topology wins and oracle assignments are close enough to plausibly be noise, for example the 0.998 ratio discussed on p. 9 and 648.35 versus 647.95 QPS in Table 4. The paper often qualifies near-ties, which is good; counting winners and reporting three-decimal normalized rankings still suggests more precision than the evidence supports. Supply reproducible configuration/results files and appropriate repeat/uncertainty evidence for distinctions the conclusions depend on. No confidence interval is needed merely to preserve an explicitly descriptive large speedup, but marginal rankings should not carry scientific weight without stability evidence.

**Presentation and scope fixes.** These are separable from the missing science.

- Define the relationship between physical-level coverage, retained seeds, residual reachability, and approximate graph recall in one place. The existing caveat about finite capacity does not address concern 1.
- Use “measured threshold crossing” consistently where tables say “equal-Recall”: the selected methods satisfy a common lower bound but may attain different actual recall. Include actual attained recall and queue capacity alongside key QPS results. The complete curves help, but the decisive Trie-base overlay curves are not provided in Appendix E, whose overlay plots use the LNG base.
- The manuscript is readable but gives substantial space to construction profiles and a weak policy while core mechanism controls are absent. A more focused paper could emphasize the entry/block design, with construction and configuration kept at the strength their evidence supports.
- For an actual PVLDB volume-20 submission, the official guideline states that appendices count toward the 12-page regular-research limit, excluding references. This supplied PDF has 23 pages, including extensive non-reference appendix material on pp. 13–23. It would need a compliant main manuscript and appropriately placed supplemental material. I reviewed all of it for scientific merit anyway; page-limit compliance is not the basis of the recommendation. I do not assess author anonymity because this artifact was supplied for an independent cold review.

**Novelty judgment and what could change the recommendation.** The distinction from UNG is concrete: the paper changes the label relation and matching entry contract, and then changes the geometric navigation unit using certified prefix blocks. The difference from Curator's shared clustering tree and temporary composite-predicate indexes is also intelligible. I do not regard familiar trie/bitmap/certificate primitives as, by themselves, a novelty objection, nor do I have evidence that the exact proposed combination has already been published. The concern is that a useful combination must demonstrate a reliable benefit and that its search semantics must be correctly characterized.

A persuasive subsequent submission would establish residual reachability or explicitly repair the LNG-base path; test the principal fixed Trie-base system on multiple datasets against strong applicable external baselines; disentangle predicate mixtures, label ordering, entry access, and topology effects; and validate construction gains at comparable output quality. It would also expose reproducible inputs/configurations and index/query-memory costs. These requirements are extensive enough that I recommend rejection of the present manuscript rather than a minor or bounded major revision. The disclosed negative results and careful method description are a good foundation for that work.
