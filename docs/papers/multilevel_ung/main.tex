\title{\system: Prefix-Frontier Entries and Predicate-Safe Block Navigation for Filtered Vector Search}

\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
Label-filtered nearest-neighbor search needs both a way to enter eligible
regions and a graph that connects them effectively. These choices are
coupled: a simpler label graph may require more entry points, while a finer
grouping can restrict geometric navigation. We present \system, a graph index
that makes this tradeoff explicit. A terminal-prefix forest pairs with a
covering entry frontier, replacing global minimal-superset reduction with
bitmap intersection and prefix-ancestor checks. Independently constructed
blocks then join exact-label groups under a shared prefix. When that prefix
contains the query labels, a block permits proximity edges across its groups
while preserving eligibility. Level-tagged search states use only the
adjacency of their authorized scale. We establish label-plane coverage and
conditional predicate safety. In an Amazon screening
study at measured Recall@10 $\geq0.90$, the prefix-base system is
2.77--18.43$\times$ faster than the optimized LNG system on three workloads
with mean selectivity below 6\%. Among 14 tested configurations, the per-workload winner has a Trie base
on seven of nine workloads; the LNG base wins near 10\% and 30\%. These results
identify complementary roles for entry simplicity and coarse navigation.
\end{abstract}
